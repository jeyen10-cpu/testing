Somewhere around the third time a friend asked me to "just send the itinerary," I realised it was easier to write the whole thing down properly. Japan is one of the more forgiving countries to plan for — the trains run on time, the signage is bilingual, and Malaysians don't even need a visa — but a good first trip still comes down to sequencing: which city first, how many nights each, and where the money actually goes. This is the version I'd hand to someone flying out of KLIA for the first time, built around ten days and four cities.

Before you book anything

The paperwork side of this trip is refreshingly light. Malaysian passport holders have been able to enter Japan visa-free for tourism since 2013, and the current allowance is up to 90 days — more than enough for a ten-day trip, with no application, no fee, and no advance registration required. You'll just need a passport valid for at least six months beyond your stay, and it helps to have your first night's accommodation and a return ticket ready to show at immigration, since those are the two things officers sometimes ask about.

A few other things are worth sorting before you leave Malaysia rather than after you land:

  • Connectivity — an eSIM bought in advance (Klook or Airalo both work fine) or a pocket WiFi counter at the arrival airport will get you online the moment you land. Don't wait to sort this out at the hotel.
  • Cash — Japan has become more card-friendly in recent years, but plenty of shrines, small ramen counters, and rural vending machines still expect yen. A 7-Eleven ATM is the easiest way to withdraw once you're there.
  • Shinkansen seats — if you're travelling in cherry blossom season (March–April) or autumn foliage season (October–November), reserve your bullet train seats a few days ahead. Unreserved cars on the Tokyo–Kyoto line do fill up.

Picking your dates

Spring and autumn get all the postcards for a reason, but they also carry the highest flight and hotel prices, and the biggest crowds at anywhere photogenic. Summer is hot and humid in a way that will feel familiar coming from Malaysia, which oddly makes it one of the more comfortable seasons for a tropical traveller, even if it's not the prettiest. Winter is the quiet, cheap option, cold but rarely miserable in Tokyo, Kyoto, or Osaka. Here's roughly how the four seasons compare on cost and crowding:

The route: ten days, four cities

The shape of this trip is the classic "Golden Route," and it's classic for a reason — it links three very different cities with a train ride short enough that you never lose a full day to transit. Direct flights from KLIA to Tokyo (Narita or Haneda) run around seven hours, which is long enough to actually sleep, and Japan sits only one hour ahead of Malaysia, so jet lag isn't really a factor. Here's how I'd lay the ten days out, one stop per day:

1
Tokyo

Arrival, and an easy first evening in Asakusa

Land, ride the airport express into the city, and check in. Rather than cramming in sights on zero sleep, I'd just walk to Senso-ji Temple and Nakamise shopping street at dusk — enough to feel like you've arrived without needing much energy for it.

Senso-ji Temple, Asakusa — Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 4.0)
2
Tokyo

Akihabara by day, Shibuya and Shinjuku by night

Electronics and anime shops in Akihabara in the morning, then across town for the Shibuya Crossing scramble, the Hachiko statue, and Shinjuku's back-alley izakaya streets once the neon comes on.

Shibuya Crossing — Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 4.0)
3
Tokyo

Harajuku, Meiji Shrine, and a change of pace

A quiet forest walk into Meiji Jingu, then straight into the noise and colour of Takeshita Street. In the afternoon, teamLab Planets or Odaiba make a good contrast — Tokyo's traditional and futuristic sides in the same day.

Meiji Shrine torii — Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 4.0)
4
Hakone

A day away from the city, chasing a view of Fuji

The Hakone Ropeway, a cruise on Lake Ashi, and — if the weather cooperates — a proper view of Mt Fuji. It's also the natural place to book an onsen soak before heading back toward Tokyo.

Mt Fuji from Lake Ashi, Hakone — Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 4.0)
5
Kyoto

Shinkansen to Kyoto, then straight to Fushimi Inari

About two and a half hours by bullet train from Tokyo. Drop your bags and head to Fushimi Inari before the light goes — the thousands of vermillion torii gates thin out noticeably by early evening.

Fushimi Inari torii path — Wikimedia Commons (public domain)
6
Kyoto

Arashiyama's bamboo grove, then Kinkaku-ji

Go to Arashiyama early — this is the one place in the itinerary where arriving before 8am genuinely changes the experience. Kinkaku-ji's golden pavilion by midday, then Nishiki Market for a grazing dinner.

Arashiyama Bamboo Grove — Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 4.0)
7
Nara

A day trip to the bowing deer and the great Buddha

Forty-five minutes from Kyoto by local train. Nara Park's free-roaming deer are the main event, but Todai-ji's wooden hall — home to a genuinely enormous bronze Buddha — is worth the trip on its own.

Deer in Nara Park — Wikimedia Commons (CC BY 4.0)
8
Osaka

Move to Osaka, castle by day, Dotonbori by night

Osaka is about fifteen minutes from Kyoto, so this is a light travel day. Osaka Castle in the afternoon, then Dotonbori's canal-side lights, takoyaki, and okonomiyaki once you're hungry again.

Osaka Castle — Wikimedia Commons (CC BY 2.5)
9
Osaka

Universal Studios, or a slower day trip

This is the day to pick your own pace: a full day at Universal Studios Japan and Super Nintendo World, or something quieter like Himeji Castle or a trip to Kobe.

Himeji Castle, a popular day trip from Osaka — Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 2.5)
10
Osaka → Kuala Lumpur

Last-minute shopping, then the flight home

A final walk through Shinsaibashi for souvenirs and one more convenience-store snack run, then out to Kansai International Airport for the flight back.

Dotonbori at night, Osaka — Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 3.0)

Getting around: is the JR Pass still worth it?

This used to be an easy recommendation. Since the Japan Rail Pass's price increase in October 2023, though, it's genuinely worth doing the maths before you buy — the nationwide pass now needs a fair amount of long-distance travel to pay for itself.

Option Cost (7-day, ordinary) Best for Watch out for
Nationwide JR Pass ≈ ¥50,000 / RM 1,480 Routes with 3+ long shinkansen legs, e.g. adding Hiroshima to this trip Fastest Nozomi/Mizuho trains need a paid supplement; doesn't cover subways
Kansai region pass ≈ ¥12,000–17,000 / RM 350–500 Trips that mostly stay within Kyoto–Osaka–Nara–Kobe Doesn't cover the Tokyo leg — pair it with separate Tokyo transport
Suica / ICOCA IC card Pay-as-you-go Every city subway, bus, and local train, every day of the trip Doesn't cover reserved shinkansen seats between cities
Individual tickets ≈ ¥13,000–15,000 per Tokyo↔Kyoto leg Simpler routes with only one or two intercity trips Often cheaper than the Pass for a single Tokyo–Kyoto–Osaka run

For this exact ten-day route — Tokyo, Kyoto, Osaka, no extra long legs — individual tickets or a regional pass usually beat the nationwide JR Pass. Add a Hiroshima or Kanazawa side trip and the maths tips in the Pass's favour.

Packing, coming from a tropical closet

The most common mistake I see from Malaysian travellers is packing for one climate when a ten-day trip across three cities can swing through several. A few things worth sorting before you leave:

Documents

  • Passport valid at least six months beyond your stay
  • Visit Japan Web QR code (optional, but speeds up immigration)
  • Return flight confirmation and hotel bookings
  • Travel insurance policy

Clothing

  • Breathable base layers — Japan's indoor heating and humidity can surprise you
  • A packable rain jacket; showers arrive fast and without much warning
  • Comfortable walking shoes — expect 10–15km days on foot
  • One warm layer for evenings, even outside of winter

Money and connectivity

  • Yen cash for shrines, small eateries, and vending machines
  • A card with no foreign transaction fees
  • eSIM or pocket WiFi, booked before you fly
  • A Suica or PASMO card, or the phone-wallet equivalent

A few questions people usually ask

Do I need a visa as a Malaysian?

No. Malaysian passport holders can enter Japan visa-free for tourism stays of up to 90 days, and have been able to since 2013 for ICAO-compliant biometric passports.

Is ten days really enough for Tokyo, Kyoto, and Osaka?

Yes, if you make peace with not seeing everything in each city. This itinerary gives roughly four nights around Tokyo, three in Kyoto, and two to three in Osaka — enough to see the highlights properly without the one-night-per-city sprint a lot of shorter trips fall into.

Should I buy the JR Pass before flying out?

Only if your route includes several long-distance legs. For a straightforward Tokyo–Kyoto–Osaka trip like this one, it's worth comparing the pass price against individual ticket costs first — the maths doesn't automatically favour the pass anymore.

What's the flight time from Kuala Lumpur?

Direct flights from KLIA to Tokyo run around seven hours. Osaka's Kansai Airport also has direct options, which can be worth considering if you'd rather fly into one city and out of another.

If you're building your own version of this trip, the one piece of advice that holds regardless of route: book your shinkansen seats and any must-visit restaurants a few weeks ahead if you're travelling in spring or autumn. Everything else in Japan tends to sort itself out as you go.
